# Creative references — optional starting points

`assets/` ships editable works and focused authoring studies. Read the reference
that helps the task, then borrow, adapt, combine or replace its ideas and code,
or author a new approach. Each study documents its mechanism and observation
limits; judge its suitability for the current work.

Playback links beside the studies open their actual rendered previews: MP3 for
independent music, MP4 for picture, retaining authored sound in audiovisual
references. Silent visual studies stay silent. These preview renders are browsing
material, not commissions, and are not copied into a Project. Adopting a study never
builds, uploads or goes online; the files a recipe does copy keep the licence text
that recipe declares, and each study's own `LICENSES.md` (with `rights.mjs` where
present) is its licence record
([licence handoff](craft/code-visuals.md#reuse-the-working-author-module)).

The three references in `audio/` study independent musical language: picture-book
3/4 writing, cartoon-jazz theme variation, and sparse plucked/breath writing.
They have no picture; render their WAV with `renderSoundScene`. They are not
already-synchronized scores for the similarly named upstream films.

An audiovisual reference stays whole under `code-visuals/`: the risograph ride
includes music/Foley, and the crayon page includes material friction and wash
sound driven by its visible mark growth. Their sound source files live beside
their picture, not in another study or a shared component catalogue. Use either
part, adapt it or write your own; the directories do not require a production route.

Visual study defaults and browsing videos use 1920×1080; choose other sizes for
the work. Small engineering samples described in their evidence are not output
limits. Complete works retain their accepted original movies and state their
native-media dimensions; an enlarged container does not recover source detail.

These partial references demonstrate particular mechanisms, not a complete film
or a prescribed quality ceiling. For camera, layout and event timing, use the
craft and workflow chapters below; a basic grid or geometry test is not a Look
reference.

`assets/projects/` holds complete works using a handoff layout: `movie/` and/or
`image/` for accepted outputs, `project/` for the editable Project and its inputs, and `review/`
for additional evidence. Copy a whole case into your working directory, then
continue from its `project/`; no reconstruction recipe is needed. Each README
explains its source and production-state boundaries. Sound stays with the work.

These are optional works to inspect and adapt, not fixed styles, a tool menu
or mandatory pipelines. The following recipe instructions concern partial
studies, not the complete Projects above.

Each study keeps its scene, recipe and implementation together. A recipe
declares those files, and any local rights declaration beside it, itself.

A study may be a small mechanism exercise rather than a finished work; its own
README states what it does and does not prove. Its `create.mjs` authors a **new**
Project, which is a packaging convenience, not a rule about your own work. Run it
as `<viscraft> author <skill-dir>/assets/studies/<area>/<study>/create.mjs ./my-<name>`,
where `<skill-dir>` is the root of the installed Skill (the directory holding
`SKILL.md`) and `<viscraft>` is the selected, receipt-verified Runtime launcher.
Resolve it through [Startup checks](runtime/preflight.md#locate-the-runtime-for-this-work);
reuse a matching shared installation or an intentionally selected local one. To
adopt a mechanism in an existing Project, edit that Project's author source and
register changed files through the current API (`importAsset`, `saveProject`,
`refresh-code` for an already registered code file); do not create a second
Project identity merely to use a reference. Fonts, recordings and
module code used by a study are declared into a Project by that study's recipe;
the Project then imports its own copies, never the Skill or Runtime installation.
The image workbench ships with the Runtime and is not an authoring asset.
Not every mechanism has a curated gallery or a worked example for every
framework; an absent example is not an implemented capability.
