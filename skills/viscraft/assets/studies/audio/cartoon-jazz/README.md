# Cartoon jazz — theme variation, stride and stop-time

**When to use it** — a work needs a complete short cartoon cue, not a loop and
not a single sting: one syncopated theme that stays recognisable when the band
states it, the scene slows down, a chase compresses it, the band stops for a
physical joke, and the theme returns for a call-and-response resolution. The
reference is useful when the cue has to **get out of the way**: the stop is a
real musical rest, but the tick/spring (or woodblock/whistle) must still be
heard.

**What it is not** — not a sample library, not a recorded band, not a DAW, and
not a promise that programme synthesis sounds like an orchestra. Every voice is
generated by `synth.mjs`; a synthesised trumpet is labelled as synthesis
everywhere. There is no sample pack, no CC BY drum kit and no TTS, so the
reference is complete offline. There is also no matching performance picture:
the stop-time and foley gap are an internal musical/sound-design arrangement, not
a claim that the cue has been synchronized with an observed comedy action.

## The passage

The cue is 16 bars of 4/4 at **120 BPM**: 32 seconds, 768 frames at 24 fps,
1,536,000 samples at 48 kHz. At this tempo a beat is 0.5 s (12 frames), a bar is
2 s (48 frames) and a sixteenth is exactly 0.125 s (3 frames), so every event
lands on an exact sample.

| Section | Seconds | Bars | Material |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `theme` | 0–8 | 1–4 | the syncopated F theme: trumpet lead, clarinet octave, trombone, stride piano, tuba/banjo oompah, brushes; a slide-whistle pickup |
| `inspect` | 8–12 | 5–6 | the same opening motif augmented ×2, thin piano/tuba, woodblock glances, muted-trumpet aside |
| `chase` | 12–18 | 7–9 | the theme compressed to sixteenths (double density), tuba ostinato, off-beat piano, snare eighths, a rising run and roll |
| `bandstop` | 18–20 | 10 | **no music attacks**: three ticks and a spring boing (base) or three woodblock knocks and a slide whistle (alternate) |
| `answer` | 20–24 | 11–12 | call-and-response on the theme, with stride and oompah restored |
| `slow` | 24–30 | 13–15 | the theme augmented ×2 again, half-time harmony, clarinet or muted trumpet lead |
| `cadence` | 30–32 | 16 | tutti F chord, comic tag and ring-out |

The band stop is the reference's central fact. `rests` declares
`{ start: 18.02, end: 19.96, scope: 'music' }`. `renderScore` adds the room to
the music bus, **then** gates that bus through the rest (with a short fade before
the boundary), then mixes the dry foley bus. A music tail from before the stop
cannot ring through; the foley events inside the rest are unaffected. The
report measures both: `musicRms` is exactly zero in the rest and `foleyRms` is
non-zero.

## One theme, three paces

`THEME` is a 16-beat phrase. `themeSlice(lo, hi, { stretch })` is the single
transformation used everywhere:

- `stretch: 1` states the theme;
- `stretch: 2` augments it for `inspect` and `slow`;
- `stretch: 0.5` compresses it to sixteenths for `chase`, doubling the note
  density without replacing the pitch order.

The variants change **physical cue business and casting**, not just tempo or
seed:

| | `base` | `alternate` |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Stop cue | tick, tick, tick, spring boing | woodblock, woodblock, woodblock, slide whistle |
| Theme lead | trumpet, clarinet +12, trombone −12 | clarinet, muted trumpet −12, banjo doubling |
| Inspect | clarinet lead, trombone shadow | muted-trumpet lead, clarinet shadow |
| Chase colour | banjo tremolo | xylophone eighths |
| Answer phrases | theme 8–12 / 0–4 / 12–16 | theme 4–8 / 8–12 / 0–4 |
| Slow phrase | theme 0–4 augmented | theme 4–8 augmented |

Both variants keep the same 120 BPM, 32-second span and theme identity. The
variant difference is visible in `events` (voices, phrases and stop cue voices)
without rendering twice.

## The score contract

`buildScore(params = {})` returns:

```
{
  seconds: 32, bpm: 120, variant, transpose, gain,
  sections: [{ id, start, end }],
  events:   [{ id, section, time, duration, voice, midi?, midiTo?, gain, pan,
               role: 'music' | 'foley' }],
  rests:    [{ start, end, scope: 'music' | 'all' }],
  cues:     [{ id, time }],
  theme:    [[beat, durationInBeats, pitch], ...],
}
```

- `time` and `duration` are seconds; `midiTo` exists only for the slide whistle.
- `role` is the bus: `music` goes through the room and the rest gate; `foley`
  stays dry and survives a `scope: 'music'` rest.
- The cues are the authored sync points (`open`, `inspect`, `chase_launch`,
  `stop`, `stop_tick_a/b/c`, `stop_release`, `restart`, …). They are data, not a
  second timeline.
- `params` is exactly `{ variant: 'base' | 'alternate', transpose: integer
  −24..24, gain: 0..2 }`. Unknown keys are rejected. Transpose shifts every
  pitched music event by whole semitones; it does not change the tune's
  intervals.

`renderScore({ sampleRate, channels, frames, params, seed })` returns
`{ channels: Float32Array[], report }`. It **rebuilds the score descriptor from
`params`**; it does not accept a prebuilt descriptor, so pass the same `params`
used to author and publish the Source:

- `channels` is exactly two arrays of exactly `frames` samples.
- Local `renderScore.frames` must equal `32 × sampleRate`: it counts samples per
  channel, not picture frames, and receives the Project Source's `durationSamples`.
  The Source declares its native `sampleRate`. There is deliberately **no
  `seconds`/truncation option**; a mismatch is a hard error naming the fields to
  keep in sync.
- The report states the peak, RMS, per-section RMS, per-voice counts, rest
  music/foley RMS and whether the headroom scale was applied.
- A peak above 0.98 is scaled down as a whole programme (authoring headroom, not
  a limiter); the Runtime never clamps.

`window.viscraftSound.render` is called **exactly once** with
`{ sampleRate, channels, samples, params, seed }`, where `samples` is a
per-channel sample span rather than a picture frame count. It contains no video
contract and fetches nothing. It returns only the contract object and leaves the
score/report on the global for inspection.

## Programmatic voices

All voices are in `synth.mjs`, all seeded with `mulberry32`, and all return a
normalised mono signal:

| Family | Voices | Method |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Stride band | `piano`, `tuba`, `banjo`, `trumpet`, `trumpetMuted`, `trombone`, `clarinet`, `xylophone` | additive harmonic stacks, one-pole filters, Karplus-Strong, inharmonic bar partials |
| Rhythm | `brush`, `snare`, `bassDrum`, `cymbal` | band-passed seeded noise, pitch-dropping sine, metallic partials |
| Comedy foley | `tick`, `woodblock`, `boing`, `slideWhistle` | resonant modes, FM chirp, exponential glide with breath |

The room is four damped combs plus two allpasses on a mono send; it is
synthesis, not convolution and not a claim about a real room. It is applied to
the music bus only.

## Author, export and render

The recipe authors a **new** Project only; the Runtime refuses an existing
directory:

```bash
<viscraft> author <skill-dir>/assets/studies/audio/cartoon-jazz/create.mjs ./my-cartoon-jazz \
  --options cartoon-options.json
```

The Project is a valid **sound-only** Project: one `browser` `sound` Source and
one `master` sound target, with no composition, picture Source or FPS. That is
not an unfinished picture; it is the delivery target. `<viscraft> export
<project> <destination>` wraps `exportProject`: it produces a portable copy
without a Build and carries the code, `LICENSES.md` and the README. Both
authoring and export are pure author-side routes; neither renders audio nor
submits a provider job.

To produce the WAV, build the declared target:

```bash
<viscraft> build ./my-cartoon-jazz --target master
```

This produces a real `medium: 'sound'` result with a PCM16LE WAV of exactly
1,536,000 samples per channel at 48 kHz; `export` (or `exportProject`) carries
the editable `project/` for handoff. A score slate, a video Source or a movie is
optional and not required by this reference. The standalone `renderSoundScene`
SDK entry still exists, but it is not the reference's standard route and
registers no Take.

## Editing

- **Variant / transpose / gain** are the Source's `params` and the recipe's
  options. They are the only parameters; there is no tempo or duration knob.
- **Length**: the score plan is 32 s / 768 frames on its internal 24 fps grid.
  The Project stores no composition; set the Source `durationSamples` and the
  `master` target `outputSamples` to `round(32 * sampleRate)` — 1,536,000 at
  48 kHz. If the score is re-authored to another length, update
  `DURATION_SECONDS` and `SOURCE_FRAMES` in `score.mjs`, the Source
  `durationSamples` and the target `outputSamples` together. Never pad or trim at
  render time.
- **Arrangement**: edit `THEME`, `THEME_CHORDS`, `SECTION_PLAN`, `RESTS`,
  `MIX_LEVEL` and the section builders. Keep `themeSlice` as the only
  augmentation/diminution path so the theme identity survives a speed change.
- **Stop**: if the rest is moved, move the foley events and cues with it. A
  `scope: 'music'` rest must still contain only foley; a `scope: 'all'` rest is
  available when the cue itself should vanish.
- After editing a registered code file, re-record its bytes with
  `<viscraft> refresh-code <project> <asset-id>`; do not hand-edit hashes.

## What the report does not say

The report says the render is non-silent, exactly spanned, unclipped, and that
the music bus is silent during the rest while foley remains. It says nothing
about whether the cue is funny, well voiced or musically satisfying: that needs
human listening.
