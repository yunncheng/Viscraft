# storybook-score — a structured 3/4 picture-book cue

**When to use it** — a work needs a structured, editable cue rather than a single
hit or a DSP demonstration: a 3/4 picture-book score with an opening motif, a
playful toy/reed change, a real silent rest, a four-bar theme stated in full, a
cadence and a winding-down coda. This reference is a pure sound Project: one
`browser` `sound` Source and one `master` sound target, so
`build --target master` produces the WAV, and the Project can still be paired
with any picture edit later. The section form is complete; that is a structural
claim, not a claim of a finished production, mix or listened musical quality.

**What it is not** — not a sample library, not a film with a hidden slate, and
not a claim that synthesised instruments are recordings. All five voices are
generated by `synth.mjs`; no audio sample, fetch, model or network call is used.
The recognizable four-bar theme is an upstream MIT `THEME` (see
`LICENSES.md`), transposed and re-orchestrated here; the 40 s form,
answer phrases, alternate orchestration and all timings are original.

## Files

| File | Role |
| --- | --- |
| `score.mjs` | The arrangement and renderer: form, theme, sections, dynamics, answer phrases, rest, coda clock, descriptor and exact-span render. No DOM, no imports outside this folder. |
| `synth.mjs` | Deterministic synthesis for music box, toy piano, flute, reed and harp, plus the small Schroeder room. No samples. |
| `sound.html` | The sound entry. Loads `score.mjs`, exposes `window.viscraftSound` and forwards `render` once with `{ sampleRate, channels, samples, params, seed }`, where `samples` is a per-channel sample span, not a picture frame count. Sound only: no `window.viscraftvideo`. |
| `create.mjs` | The editable recipe: validates the options, preflights the descriptor and declares a pure sound Project with its files, assets and handoffs. |
| `README.md` | This reference note. |
| `LICENSES.md` | Full MIT text and the actual adopted upstream segments. Travels with the Project. |

The three code files are the only Assets; `README.md` is a `readme` handoff and
the licence travels as a `license` handoff.

## Form (40.0 s, 16 bars, 3/4, 72 BPM, base key F major)

| Section | Seconds | Material |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `opening` | 0.0–5.0 | music-box motif alone; the theme in octaves; role `motif` |
| `toys` | 5.0–12.5 | toy piano + reed change, turning accents, rising answer, reed wind-down; role `reorchestration` |
| `rest` | 12.5–15.0 | one full bar with no new attacks; every bus is gated; role `rest` |
| `theme` | 15.0–25.0 | complete four-bar theme: flute lead, music-box doubling, reed counter-line, harp bass; role `theme` |
| `cadence` | 25.0–27.5 | cadence in the sounding key and long bass; role `cadence` |
| `coda` | 27.5–40.0 | music-box answer with playback rate 1 → 0.93, then room ring-out; role `winddown` |

The base key is F major. `transpose` shifts every MIDI note and the descriptor
reports the sounding `key` and the unchanged `baseKey` separately.

The coda is a real resample of a stereo source buffer. The read position advances
by a rate that falls from 1 to 0.93, so later onsets are farther apart **and** the
same pitch is lower; it is not a volume fade. The descriptor reports both the
source notes and the measured post-resample `time`/`rate` of every coda event.

## Contract

`buildScore(params = {})` accepts only `variant` (`"base"` | `"alternate"`),
`transpose` (integer semitones, −12..12) and `gain` (0..1). Any other key is a
loud error; there is no `seconds` option that could silently cut a phrase. It
returns sections, events, rests, cues, stems, section dynamics, `meter`
(`"3/4"`), `meterSpec` (`{beats:3,unit:4}`), `meterLabel`, `baseKey`/`key`, and
the coda source/map. Every event and section carries a `role`: `motif`,
`reorchestration`, `rest`, `theme`, `cadence` or `winddown`.

`renderScore({ sampleRate, channels, frames, params, seed })` returns
`{ channels, report }` with exactly `channels` (1 or 2) arrays of exactly
`frames` samples. The sample rate is the synthesis clock; `frames` is the
authoritative span. A span shorter than the final phrase and its ring is refused
with the exact fields to update, never padded, trimmed or silently truncated.
There is no wall clock, `Math.random`, network access or Web Audio graph; the
same inputs produce the same bytes in the same browser build.

The mix is peak-normalised to 0.92 only when the raw sum exceeds it, and the
optional `gain` is applied after that. The report discloses `rawPeak`,
`normalisationScale` and `preGainPeak`, then the delivered `peak`; `|sample| <= 1`
is guaranteed without a limiter. `report.stems` are **raw dry voice buses**
before the room send, rest gate, tail fade, normalisation and gain, as stated by
`stemsScope`; they are authoring meters, not delivered stems. The section RMS
values are measured on the delivered channels. None of this is an acceptance and
it says nothing about whether the music is good.


## The two variants

- **base** — music-box opening; toy/reed oom-pah-pah; the rising answer
  C5–D5–E5–F5; flute theme with music-box doubling, reed counter-line and
  harp root–fifth–octave bass; music-box cadence and coda.
- **alternate** — a reed drone under the opening; the toy and music box swap
  roles in `toys`; a different answer (C5–E5–G5–Bb5) at a different start time;
  music-box broken chords and a descending reed counter-line in the theme; a harp
  walking bass; a toy-piano cadence; a toy-doubled coda. Cues differ too (an extra
  `turn`, a shifted `answer`, a different `last`). The same 3/4 theme, rest and
  rate 1 → 0.93 coda remain.

A change of seed or gain is not a content variant; both variants keep the same
musical identity.

## Author and render

```bash
# 1. Author a NEW pure sound Project.
<viscraft> author <skill-dir>/assets/studies/audio/storybook-score/create.mjs ./my-storybook-score \
  --options storybook-options.json

# 2. Build the declared sound target.
<viscraft> build ./my-storybook-score --target master
```

`storybook-options.json` may contain `variant`, `transpose` and `gain` only.
The Project has no composition, picture raster or FPS: the Source renders
natively at 48 kHz over 1,920,000 samples, and the `master` target declares the
same rate and span. Build produces a real `medium: 'sound'` result with its
PCM16LE WAV; `export` (or `exportProject`) carries the editable `project/` for
handoff. The standalone `renderSoundScene` SDK entry still exists, but it is
not this reference's standard route and registers no Take.

The built Project carries `project.json`, both modules, `sound.html`, the
README, this licence and the Runtime notice; it can be reopened and the same
target rebuilt without a picture Source.

## Editing

- Music: `SECTIONS`, `THEME`, the answer arrays, the counter-lines, the cadence
  and `codaNotes` in `score.mjs`. Keep new events in seconds on the same clock.
- Balance: section `dynamics` are per voice; `STEM_GAINS` are per stem. They are
  applied separately, so rebalancing a stem does not rewrite the events.
- Instruments: `synth.mjs`. Changing it affects future renders of the Project.
- The form is fixed at 40 s, which is 1,920,000 samples at 48 kHz. To change
  length, edit `BARS`/`SECONDS` in `score.mjs` and the authored section/coda
  constants together, then set the Source `durationSamples` to
  `round(SECONDS * 48000)` and the `master` target's `outputSamples` to the same
  span. There is no picture fps or `composition.outputFrames` to update. The
  recipe has no length option on purpose.
- After editing a registered code file, use
  `<viscraft> refresh-code <project-directory> <asset-id>`; do not hand-edit hashes
  or revisions.

## Score structure

At 48 kHz the 40 s form is exactly 1,920,000 samples per channel, and the rest is
digital silence in both channels. The coda's F5 energy moves from about 698 Hz to
about 650 Hz while its onsets spread — both follow from the same rate falling to
0.93. The raw summed peak is about 1.084, so the renderer normalises by about
0.849 to 0.92 before the optional gain; the report exposes `rawPeak`,
`normalisationScale` and `preGainPeak`, and labels the stem meters as raw
pre-master buses. An isolated harp F2 peaks at 87.5 Hz against the intended
87.31 Hz, and a sustained flute C5 keeps about 522.5 zero-crossing Hz across
early, middle and late windows — a bounded vibrato. Delivered levels for the base
variant are peak 0.91998 and RMS 0.09210, with section RMS opening 0.0752, toys
0.0652, theme 0.1456, cadence 0.1425 and coda 0.0363; the alternate variant
(transpose −3, gain 0.8) delivers peak 0.7360 and RMS 0.08552. Listening is the
acceptance step for the music; these numbers do not judge it.
